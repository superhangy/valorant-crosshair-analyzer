Crosshair Discipline Analyzer — findings

A pro's crosshair is already on the head before the fight starts.

Across 76 hand-reviewed radiant / pro VODs, the median enemy becomes visible just 3.1% of a screen-width from where the crosshair already sits — close enough to be a click, not a flick. This is the baseline every one of my own matches gets measured against.

The metric is deliberately narrow: at the exact frame an enemy head first appears, how far is it from screen-centre (Valorant always renders the crosshair there)? A fine-tuned head detector finds the head, a second body model cross-checks it, and every surviving frame is reviewed by hand before it counts. 2,145 engagements cleared that gate.

Three things the data settles

Findings.

+0.2%

Smoke on the sightline barely moves pre-aim.

Reveals through or beside a smoke (n=805) have a median of 3.15% vs 2.98% on a clear sightline (n=1,340). The intuition that smokes wreck crosshair placement doesn't hold — pros still pre-aim the common angle whether or not they can see it.

3.1 ≈ 2.9

Duelist vs. support role predicts almost nothing.

Duelist engagements (n=1,204) median 3.14%; non-duelists (n=862) median 2.93%. Entry-fragging duelists peek more often, but they don't pre-aim any looser doing it. Crosshair discipline is a personal skill, not an agent property.

7.3%

Open-map angles cost roughly double.

Ascent (median 4.8%) and Breeze (4.0%) run well above the tight angles of Split (2.4%) and Sunset (2.2%). Long sightlines mean more possible enemy positions to split the crosshair between — the same player pre-aims worse simply because the map gives them more to cover.

    How it's built

    From a raw VOD to one number.

    01 Sample

    Each VOD is walked at 30 fps with OpenCV — fine enough to catch a reveal inside a 200 ms reaction window.

    02 Detect

    A YOLOv8 head detector fine-tuned on Valorant frames finds candidate heads; a separately-trained body model must agree, which kills most viewmodel and geometry false-positives.

    03 Filter

    OCR and heuristic gates drop spectate mode, the scoreboard, kill-cams, buy phase and the match-end screen before anything is scored.

    04 Reveal & measure

    The first frame a head appears after being absent is the reveal. Distance from frame-centre to the head box is recorded as a percent of screen width.

    05 Hand review

    Every surviving frame is clicked through by a human and labelled good or one of nine false-positive categories. Raw output is never used directly.

    06 Pool & compare

    The 76 reviewed CSVs are aggregated here. My own recorded matches run the identical pipeline, then sit next to this baseline.

    Known limits

    • Distance is 2D screen distance, not angular error — a far enemy and a near one at the same pixel gap score alike.
    • Map is parsed from video titles; only 14 of 76 VODs name one, so per-map n is small.
    • The head detector's own hard-negative retrain collapsed three times — the hand-review gate exists because the detector alone can't be trusted.
    • Teammate-as-target is the dominant false-positive; a trained classifier now pre-flags it at ~96% but every frame is still reviewed.
    • This is a repost/compilation channel — footage is real ranked/pro play but not the players' own uploads.
